# AI rewrites what England's school subjects are for

The research available to October 2026 shows AI **re-weighting** the skills in England's 14 statutory subjects much more than replacing them. AI is taking over specific *outputs*: routine prose, transactional translation, junior-level coding, stock illustration and background music. The same evidence raises the value of verification, judgement, embodied practice and interpersonal skill, which people need in order to use AI well. The strongest learning evidence is a warning as well as a promise. In a field experiment, unrestricted GPT-4 raised practice scores, but students then scored **17% lower** than controls on an unaided maths exam. A guard-railed tutor avoided that harm ([Bastani et al., PNAS 2025](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122)). So a skill AI can perform still has to be learned by humans who will supervise AI. In the labour market, measured effects so far are narrow. There is **no economy-wide job loss**, but a consistent fall in junior hiring in AI-exposed white-collar roles: US 22–25-year-olds in the most exposed jobs are 19% below trend ([Stanford Digital Economy Lab](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publications/canaries-in-the-coal-mine/)). Whether AI is the main cause is still disputed. The subjects whose core is producing text, code or images (English composition, computing, languages, art, music composition) face the most change. Those whose core is physical or relational (PE, D&T making, food, RSHE) face the least. England's response through the Curriculum and Assessment Review (CAR) keeps the existing subject structure. It adds AI literacy to computing and media literacy to citizenship and English, and keeps exams as the main form of assessment partly because of AI. The new curriculum is first taught in September 2028 ([Government response, Nov 2025](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/690b2a4a14b040dfe82922ea/Government_response_to_the_Curriculum_and_Assessment_Review.pdf)). Beyond about 2036, nothing in this report is a forecast. AI researchers give a 50% chance that machines outperform humans at every task by 2047, but put the same odds on full automation of all occupations only by 2116 ([Grace et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02843)). With a range that wide, the 20- and 30-year outlooks are scenarios, and the sound planning approach is to teach skills that stay valuable whichever scenario arrives.

## England's curriculum is being rebuilt for 2028 on evidence only four years deep

### Scope: the national curriculum is England's

Strictly, the "national curriculum" belongs to England alone. At KS1–2 the compulsory subjects are English, maths, science, design and technology (D&T), history, geography, art and design, music, PE (including swimming), computing, and a language at KS2 ([GOV.UK KS1–2](https://www.gov.uk/national-curriculum/key-stage-1-and-2)). KS3 adds citizenship, and modern foreign languages (MFL) become compulsory. At KS4 the core is English, maths and science, plus compulsory computing, PE and citizenship. Schools must also offer at least one subject in each of four entitlement areas: arts, D&T, humanities and MFL ([GOV.UK KS3–4](https://www.gov.uk/national-curriculum/key-stage-3-and-4)). **RE and RSHE are statutory but sit outside the national curriculum.** Parents may withdraw children from RE and sex education. The government will bring RE into the national curriculum only if the sector reaches "a clear shared position" on its future ([Government response](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/690b2a4a14b040dfe82922ea/Government_response_to_the_Curriculum_and_Assessment_Review.pdf)).

The other UK nations have their own curricula and treat AI mainly as a cross-curricular safeguarding and competence issue. **Scotland** (Curriculum for Excellence) published AI "guidelines and guardrails" on 25 March 2026. They state that AI "must not, in any way, diminish human-centred education" ([Scottish Government](https://www.gov.scot/publications/scottish-guidelines-guardrails-use-artificial-intelligence-ai-schools/pages/2/)). **Wales** (Curriculum for Wales) has a cross-curricular Digital Competence Framework. Estyn's 2026 thematic review found most schools "still in the early stages" with AI, and found teachers reporting substantial workload cuts. It recommended updating the framework to include AI literacy ([Estyn](https://annual-report.estyn.gov.wales/app/uploads/2025/12/a-new-era-how-artificial-intelligence-ai-is-supporting-teaching-and-learning-2026-02-11.pdf)). **Northern Ireland** (NI Curriculum, CCEA) has an Assembly research paper on generative AI in education ([NI Assembly RaISe](https://www.niassembly.gov.uk/globalassets/documents/raise/publications/2022-2027/2025/education/2725.pdf)). The research did not check NI's school-level AI guidance. In PISA 2025's first "Learning in the Digital World" assessment, England scored 525, Scotland 515, Northern Ireland 513 and Wales 501 ([Scottish Government PISA report](https://www.gov.scot/publications/pisa-2025-national-report-scotland/pages/8/)).

### The 2025 review changes emphasis, not architecture

The Curriculum and Assessment Review, chaired by Becky Francis, reported on 5 November 2025 and described its approach as "evolution, not revolution". The government kept "the existing structural architecture of subjects, key stages". It committed to teaching "AI literacy, how to use data… as well as learning about bias in technology" within computing, replacing GCSE Computer Science with a broader Computing GCSE, and exploring a level 3 qualification in data science and AI. Citizenship becomes statutory from KS1, carrying media and financial literacy. EBacc accountability measures are removed. The revised curriculum is due in spring 2027 for first teaching in **September 2028**, reformed GCSEs follow in 2029–30, and AS/A levels in 2031–32 ([Government response](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/690b2a4a14b040dfe82922ea/Government_response_to_the_Curriculum_and_Assessment_Review.pdf)). The review also ruled that "there should not be any expansion of written coursework" because of the risk generative AI poses to non-exam assessment ([CAR final report](https://data.parliament.uk/DepositedPapers/Files/DEP2025-0723/Curriculum_and_Assessment_Review_final_report.pdf)). The research found no evidence that draft programmes of study had been published by October 2026, so whether the spring 2027 date will hold is unknown.

### How to read the evidence in this report

The evidence is uneven, so claims in this report are tagged by type:

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[EMP]** | Empirical observation of real outcomes: trials, payroll or job-postings data, official statistics |
| **[MODEL]** | Model-based estimate of exposure, technical potential or a projection. Never a measurement of job loss |
| **[SURVEY]** | Opinion of employers, workers or experts |
| **[SPEC]** | Scenario or informed speculation |
| **[VENDOR]** | Produced or funded by a firm selling AI or consulting |
| **[ADVOCACY]** | Produced by a body with an institutional interest in the subject |
| **†** | A figure carried from the research notes that was **not re-checked against the primary source** during research. Spot-check before quoting |

One rule runs through the whole report: **exposure is not job loss**. The UK Department for Education's own exposure study says it "does not distinguish whether a job will be augmented or replaced" ([DfE 2023](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/656856b8cc1ec500138eef49/Gov.UK_Impact_of_AI_on_UK_Jobs_and_Training.pdf)).

On time horizons: hard evidence covers roughly 2022–2026. Official projections reach about ten years. Nothing credible forecasts demand for specific school-subject skills at 20 or 30 years. That matters because the timescales are long: a child starting Reception in 2026 sits GCSEs around 2037 and reaches mid-career around 2056 ([Cognito summary](https://cognito.org/blog/gcse-curriculum-review-2026-whats-on-the-table)).

## Exposure is broad, realised job loss is narrow, and the squeeze falls on junior roles

### Forecasts agree on rising skills, but rest mostly on employer opinion

The main skills forecasts agree with one another. The WEF *Future of Jobs Report 2025* **[SURVEY]**, which surveyed more than 1,000 large employers, ranks analytical thinking as the top core skill. AI and big data, technological literacy, creative thinking, resilience, and curiosity and lifelong learning are the fastest-growing skills. "Manual dexterity, endurance and precision" shows a net decline. "Reading, writing and mathematics" are expected to see only a *small* net decline and sit among the **most stable** skills, which means they stop differentiating candidates rather than becoming obsolete ([WEF FoJ 2025, ch. 3](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/in-full/3-skills-outlook/)). WEF's headline that 39% of core skills will change by 2030 fell from 44% two years earlier, a reminder that these are shifting expectations rather than measurements† ([WEF](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/)).

The key UK source is NFER's *Skills Imperative 2035* **[MODEL + SURVEY]**. It projects that **1–3 million UK jobs** in declining occupations, mainly administrative, secretarial, customer service and machine operation, could disappear by 2035, "largely due to AI and automation". Overall employment still grows, mainly in professional roles that make heavy use of six "essential employment skills": communication, collaboration, problem-solving, organising, creative thinking and information literacy ([NFER, Nov 2025](https://www.nfer.ac.uk/press-releases/up-to-three-million-uk-jobs-at-risk-over-the-next-decade-says-report/)). NFER also finds that these essential skills are "very similar" to those measured in 2010 and 2020 ([HEPI on NFER](https://www.hepi.ac.uk/?p=24236)). **Rising demand comes from more people needing the same skills, not from new kinds of skill.** McKinsey **[MODEL; VENDOR]** reaches a similar conclusion: over 70% of skills employers seek appear in both automatable and non-automatable work, and demand for "AI fluency" in US job postings grew about sevenfold in two years ([MGI 2025](https://www.mckinsey.com/mgi/our-research/agents-robots-and-us-skill-partnerships-in-the-age-of-ai)). LinkedIn's claim that 70% of job skills will change by 2030 comes from a Microsoft-owned vendor and was not verified† ([LinkedIn](https://economicgraph.linkedin.com/research/work-change-report)). Skills England found employers unclear about what "AI skills" even are. The government's target of 7.5 million workers with AI skills by 2030 runs through partnerships with NVIDIA, Google, IBM and Microsoft **[VENDOR partnership]** ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/help-for-uk-businesses-to-fill-400bn-ai-skills-gap)).

### Generative AI reverses the old automation pattern

Exposure studies **[MODEL]** consistently find that generative AI reaches **cognitive, professional and clerical work**, and more-educated, better-paid workers, which is the reverse of earlier automation. DfE's 2023 analysis ranks management consultants, accountants and psychologists as most exposed, along with **teaching**. It finds that **economics and mathematics degrees** lead to the most exposed jobs. Roofers, steel erectors and sports players are least exposed ([DfE 2023](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/656856b8cc1ec500138eef49/Gov.UK_Impact_of_AI_on_UK_Jobs_and_Training.pdf)). The ILO's 2025 index puts 1 in 4 workers globally in occupations with some exposure, but only 3.3% in the highest band. Women are far more exposed than men in high-income countries (9.6% vs 3.5%), and the ILO concludes "job transformation is the most likely impact" ([ILO WP140](https://www.ilo.org/publications/generative-ai-and-jobs-refined-global-index-occupational-exposure)).

Headline exposure figures vary enormously:

| Source | Estimate | Note |
|---|---|---|
| IPPR | 11% of UK tasks exposed to current AI; 59% in a "second wave" | Scenarios run from 7.9m UK jobs lost to none, depending on policy ([IPPR](https://www.ippr.org/media-office/up-to-8-million-uk-jobs-at-risk-from-ai-unless-government-acts-finds-ippr)) |
| Eloundou et al. | ~80% of US workers have at least 10% of tasks exposed | OpenAI co-authors **[VENDOR]**; science- and critical-thinking-heavy tasks are *less* exposed† ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10130)) |
| McKinsey | Demonstrated technology could in theory automate 57% of US work hours | Explicitly "not a forecast of job losses" ([MGI 2025](https://www.mckinsey.com/mgi/our-research/agents-robots-and-us-skill-partnerships-in-the-age-of-ai)) |

The cautionary precedent is Frey and Osborne's 2013 estimate that 47% of US jobs were at "high risk". It never materialised as job loss†. A task-based re-analysis cut the figure to 9%† ([Oxford Martin](https://www.oxfordmartin.ox.ac.uk/publications/the-future-of-employment); [OECD 2016](https://doi.org/10.1787/5jlz9h56dvq7-en)). Acemoglu's sceptical estimate **[MODEL]** is that only about 4.6% of US tasks are profitably automatable within ten years, adding about 1% to GDP† ([NBER w32487](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32487)). The gap between that and vendor estimates is itself evidence of how uncertain this is.

### What has actually happened: entry-level hiring, not mass layoffs

The realised evidence **[EMP]** to October 2026 shows **no general rise in unemployment** among exposed workers, but a repeated signal of reduced junior hiring. Stanford's study of ADP payroll data, revised in August 2026, finds employment of 22–25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed occupations **19% below** the level it would have reached had it tracked less-exposed peers. That gap is up from about 13% in the first version. The effect runs through fewer hires rather than layoffs. It is concentrated where AI *substitutes* for tasks, and employment is flat or rising where AI *complements* them. The authors call the result descriptive, not causal, and note that it weakens when education is controlled for ([Stanford DEL](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publications/canaries-in-the-coal-mine/)). Anthropic's analysis of US Current Population Survey data **[VENDOR]** finds no systematic unemployment rise. Job-finding rates for 22–25-year-olds entering exposed occupations fell about 14%, a result it describes as "barely" significant ([Anthropic, Mar 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/labor-market-impacts)). Danish administrative data show "precise zeros" for earnings and hours effects in 2023–24† ([NBER w33777](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33777)).

The UK evidence points the same way but rests on weaker data. A King's College London working paper based on job postings finds that highly AI-exposed firms cut employment by 4.5%, and junior roles by 5.8%. Postings in highly exposed occupations fell 23.4%, while **customer-facing roles rose slightly** ([KCL](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/news/new-study-reveals-early-impact-of-ai-on-job-market-in-uk)). A 41-country follow-up (not yet peer-reviewed) associates firm adoption of AI with **+6.7% senior and −2.5% junior** employment and slightly higher total recruitment. On that reading AI changes the mix of jobs within firms rather than shrinking them ([Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-10-ai-linked-employment-globally.html)). Adzuna data show UK graduate vacancies down about 45.6% year on year in July 2026 (secondary source; verify against Adzuna). However, CFO surveys name cost control rather than AI as the main brake ([UK Fact Check](https://www.ukfactcheck.com/article/418/graduate-job-opportunities-have-fallen-by-almost-50-in-just-one-year-as-employers-increasingly-replace-entry-level-roles-with-ai)). A Work Foundation survey **[SURVEY]** found 36% of UK firms had cut entry-level jobs, and 58–60% of the medium and large firms among them cited AI ([Zawya/Reuters](https://www.zawya.com/en/world/global/one-in-three-uk-employers-have-cut-entry-level-jobs-survey-shows-467391)). **The pattern itself is reasonably well established. Confidence that AI is its main cause is low to moderate.**

### Augmentation helps novices most, and that has a cost

Controlled trials **[EMP]** show large productivity gains on tasks within AI's capability:

| Setting | Gain with AI | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Customer support | About 14% overall; about 34% for novices† | [NBER w31161](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161) |
| Professional writing | About 40% less time; about 18% higher quality† | [Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adh2586) |
| BCG consulting | 25% faster; more than 40% higher quality† | [HBS](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=64700) |

The BCG study also found that on a task *outside* the AI's capability, consultants using AI were **19 percentage points less likely to get it right**†. That is the "jagged frontier". In METR's trial, experienced developers working in their own mature codebases were **19% slower** with AI, even though they believed it had sped them up ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/)). The first clinical evidence of deskilling is observational. After AI-assisted colonoscopy was introduced, endoscopists' *unassisted* adenoma detection rate fell from 28.4% to 22.4% ([Warwick WRAP](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/191005)).

These results connect. AI lifts novices the most, which is exactly why firms may need fewer novices. Schools should take the same lesson from the jagged-frontier and deskilling findings: domain knowledge and the ability to verify AI output are what stop people being misled when AI fails.

### Social skill has the best evidence of lasting value; creativity is contested

Among "human" skills, **social and interpersonal skills** have the strongest evidence. Jobs requiring heavy social interaction grew by nearly 12 percentage points as a share of US employment between 1980 and 2012, and returns were highest where social and cognitive skills were combined **[EMP]** ([Deming, QJE 2017](https://scholar.harvard.edu/sites/scholar.harvard.edu/files/ddeming/files/deming_socialskills_qje.pdf)). Care and assisting work scores lowest for AI applicability in Microsoft's analysis of Copilot conversations **[VENDOR]** ([arXiv 2507.07935](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07935v6)).

The case for creativity is weaker than often claimed. WEF lists creative thinking as fast-growing **[SURVEY]**, but AI improves writing and ideation performance in trials. Experts also expected AI to produce a song indistinguishable from a popular musician's new release by 2028 ([Grace et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02843)). "Creative thinking with and about AI output" is a defensible goal. "Creativity is AI-proof" is not.

### Beyond ten years, expert forecasts are too wide to plan on

Three bodies of work frame the long horizon:

- **Expert survey [SURVEY].** Grace et al. surveyed 2,778 AI researchers in late 2023. They give a 50% chance by **2047** of machines outperforming humans at every task. For full automation of all occupations, they give 10% by 2037 and 50% by **2116**. These dates moved 13 and 48 years earlier respectively in a single year, and individual answers vary enormously ([arXiv 2401.02843](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02843)).
- **Consultancy projection [MODEL; VENDOR].** McKinsey's 2023 midpoint for automating half of today's work activities is about 2045† ([MGI 2023](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/mckinsey-digital/our-insights/the-economic-potential-of-generative-ai-the-next-productivity-frontier)).
- **Historical evidence [EMP].** About 60% of US employment in 2018 was in job titles that did not exist in 1940† ([Autor et al., QJE 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjae008)). Whether that pattern holds for a technology aimed at cognition itself is unknown.

The government-commissioned *International AI Safety Report 2026* describes the evidence as mixed and the outlook as highly uncertain ([IASR 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.21012)).

The research found **no expert forecast of school-relevant skills beyond 2035**. The table below summarises where each family of skills stands.

| Skill family | What the evidence shows now | 10-year direction | 20–30 years [SPEC] | Evidence base |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Literacy | High AI overlap on writing; WEF rates it "stable" | Augmented; still the foundation for judging AI output | Routine writing largely automated; literacy persists as a foundation | Moderate / low |
| Numeracy | Maths and economics graduates in the most exposed jobs | Augmented; verification needed | As for literacy | Moderate |
| Scientific reasoning and critical thinking | Lower LLM exposure; top WEF skill | More valuable | Likely valuable | Moderate (mostly survey) |
| Coding | Highest AI usage; UK technical postings fell most | Transformed; junior entry squeezed | Highly uncertain | Moderate that disruption is happening |
| Creativity | Forecast to grow, but AI is strong at ideation | Augmented; contested | Uncertain | Low |
| Languages | Translation among the most AI-applicable tasks | Partly replaced (transactional use) | Uncertain | Low–moderate |
| Physical and manual | Least exposed to generative AI; WEF net decline | Needed | Depends on robotics | Moderate / low |
| Interpersonal and social | Rising returns; customer-facing roles resilient | More valuable | Most robust bet | Moderate–high |

Sources: [Eloundou](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10130)†; [DfE 2023](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/656856b8cc1ec500138eef49/Gov.UK_Impact_of_AI_on_UK_Jobs_and_Training.pdf); [WEF](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/in-full/3-skills-outlook/); [KCL](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/news/new-study-reveals-early-impact-of-ai-on-job-market-in-uk); [Deming](https://scholar.harvard.edu/sites/scholar.harvard.edu/files/ddeming/files/deming_socialskills_qje.pdf).

## In learning, how an AI tool is designed decides whether pupils gain or lose

The labour-market evidence describes what AI can do. The learning evidence shows what happens when pupils use it. The best causal study is **Bastani et al. [EMP, RCT, about 1,000 Turkish high-school students]**. A ChatGPT-like tutor raised practice performance by 48%, but once access was removed students scored **17% lower** than controls on an unaided exam. A tutor with guardrails (hints rather than answers) raised practice performance by 127% and avoided the harm, but produced no exam gain over controls ([PNAS 2025](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122)).

Well-designed tutors show large short-term gains:

- **Harvard physics [EMP].** In a crossover trial (N = 194), a scaffolded GPT-4 tutor produced **0.73–1.3 SD** larger learning gains than in-class active learning, in less time. The authors note ceiling effects, only two topics, and a heavily engineered tutor ([Kestin et al., Sci Rep 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12179260/)). A blog re-analysis, not peer-reviewed, puts the gain ratio at 9–24% rather than "more than double" ([Clay Ford](https://www.clayford.net/posts/ai_tutoring_nature/)).
- **Nigeria English [EMP].** A World Bank RCT (N = 759) found **+0.23 SD in English** after six weeks of teacher-facilitated sessions using Copilot. It is a working paper, and the control group received no extra tuition time ([World Bank](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/15e1ff08-15ae-4f7a-b2a8-d146e6c113ee)).
- **Teacher workload, England [EMP].** The EEF/NFER trial is the only English RCT here, and it measures teacher workload rather than pupil learning. ChatGPT with a guide cut KS3 science planning time by **31%** (25 minutes a week) with no detectable fall in resource quality ([EEF](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/teachers-using-chatgpt-alongside-a-guide-to-support-them-to-use-it-effectively-can-cut-lesson-planning-time-by-over-30-per-cent)).

Evidence on "cognitive offloading" is weaker:

| Study | Type | Finding | Caveat | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gerlich, n = 666, UK | Correlational survey | Frequent AI use linked to lower critical thinking | Partly self-report | [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4698/15/1/6) |
| Lee et al., Microsoft/CMU **[VENDOR]** | Self-report survey | Higher confidence in AI linked to less critical thinking effort | Adults, self-report | [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-impact-of-generative-ai-on-critical-thinking-self-reported-reductions-in-cognitive-effort-and-confidence-effects-from-a-survey-of-knowledge-workers/) |
| MIT "Your Brain on ChatGPT", n = 54 | Preprint, EEG | Weaker neural engagement in LLM-assisted essay writers | Not peer-reviewed; a published critique questions its power and reporting | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08872); [critique](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.00856) |

The OECD's *Digital Education Outlook 2026* reportedly concludes that AI supports learning "when guided by clear teaching principles". The primary text returned an error, so this comes from secondary summaries ([EPALE](https://epale.ec.europa.eu/en/blog/future-learning-key-takeaways-oecd-digital-education-outlook-2026)).

Usage has outrun this evidence. **56% of UK 8–17-year-olds** have used AI. Of those who read AI search summaries, **46% think they are always accurate** ([Ofcom 2026](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/research-and-data/media-literacy-research/children/2026-children-and-parents-report/children-and-parents-media-use-and-attitudes-report-2025-6.pdf?v=418231)). **94% of UK undergraduates** use AI for assessed work ([HEPI 2026](https://www.hepi.ac.uk/reports/student-generative-ai-survey-2026/)). Most English teachers use AI weekly ([Teacher Tapp via Chartered College](https://my.chartered.college/?p=41701)).

Detected AI malpractice at GCSE and A level is small: **100 cases in 2025**, which is 75% of plagiarism cases but only 2% of all malpractice ([Ofqual](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/malpractice-in-gcse-as-and-a-level-summer-2025-exam-series/malpractice-in-gcse-as-and-a-level-summer-2025-exam-series)). The Chief Regulator nonetheless told exam boards in March 2026 that there is "significant concern… about the real extent of AI misuse" and demanded stricter authentication of coursework ([Ofqual letter](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69a5adc1bdca0a628ece181b/Letter_to_Exam_Board_CEOs_from_Sir_Ian_Bauckham_March_2026.pdf)). The detected cases should be read as a floor, not an estimate.

DfE policy is now explicitly cautious about pupils' use of AI. Its January 2026 product safety standards ask edtech tools to monitor and report pupils' "cognitive offloading" ([DfE standards](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/generative-ai-product-safety-standards/generative-ai-product-safety-standards)). The exact wording was seen only via a search extract. DfE is also building AI tutoring tools for up to 450,000 disadvantaged pupils, with availability planned by the end of 2027 ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/450000-disadvantaged-pupils-could-benefit-from-ai-tutoring-tools)). The research found no published results from any pupil-facing AI trial in English schools.

## Core subjects shift from producing answers to judging them

### English: composition is the most exposed strand; critical reading and oracy gain value

**Knowledge and skills.** English is statutory at KS1–4 and built on four strands: spoken language, reading, writing, and grammar and vocabulary ([DfE English PoS](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-english-programmes-of-study)). The strand structure was summarised from the 2013 document and not re-fetched. GCSE English Language is 100% exam-assessed, split evenly between reading and writing. Reading assessment includes "identifying bias and misuse of evidence". Spoken language is reported separately and carries no weight in the grade ([DfE GCSE subject content](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7bfd7640f0b63f7572aa8b/GCSE_English_language.pdf)). The CAR adds a statutory Year 8 reading test and new oracy frameworks. It also recasts GCSE English Language around "the nature and expression of language" across multimodal and "ephemeral" online texts ([Government response](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/690b2a4a14b040dfe82922ea/Government_response_to_the_Curriculum_and_Assessment_Review.pdf); [CAR](https://data.parliament.uk/DepositedPapers/Files/DEP2025-0723/Curriculum_and_Assessment_Review_final_report.pdf)).

**Risks.** The main risk is to learning rather than to grades. Writing about content is a proven learning tool. A meta-analysis of 56 experiments found an effect size of 0.30 across science, social studies and maths **[EMP]** ([Graham et al. 2020](https://acuresearchbank.acu.edu.au/item/8x2w0/the-effects-of-writing-on-learning-in-science-social-studies-and-mathematics-a-meta-analysis)). Outsourcing drafting removes that practice. The risk also lands on a reading culture already at a **20-year low**: only 32.7% of 8–18-year-olds enjoy reading in their free time ([National Literacy Trust 2025](https://literacytrust.org.uk/research-services/research-reports/children-and-young-peoples-reading-in-2025)). Among 13–18-year-olds who use generative AI monthly, 60.9% use it for homework ([NLT AI report](https://literacytrust.org.uk/research-services/research-reports/young-people-and-teachers-use-of-generative-ai-to-support-literacy-in-2025/)). That reading decline began before ChatGPT, so AI is not its cause, but it compounds it.

**Opportunities.** The best causal evidence is the Nigeria RCT (+0.23 SD in English). It comes from a low-baseline, teacher-mediated setting and should not be assumed to transfer to England ([World Bank](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/15e1ff08-15ae-4f7a-b2a8-d146e6c113ee)). Feedback on drafts at scale and text-to-speech for dyslexic and EAL learners are plausible uses, but the research found no English trial evidence for them.

**Labour market.** Freelance writing work on Upwork fell 2% in jobs and 5.2% in earnings after ChatGPT **[EMP]** ([Hui et al.](https://www.cesifo.org/DocDL/cesifo1_wp10601.pdf)). Posts for automation-prone writing and coding jobs fell 21% relative to manual jobs, and the posts that remained were more complex and better paid ([Demirci et al., Management Science 2025](https://www.diw.de/de/diw_01.c.906828.de/publikationen/externe_referierte_aufsaetze/2025_0010/who_is_ai_replacing__the_impact_of_generative_ai_on_online_freelancing_platforms.html)). Adult literacy in England (272 vs an OECD average of 260) predates generative AI, so it cannot show AI effects ([NFER on PIAAC](https://nfer.ac.uk/media/r0kpehmv/key_insights_from_the_survey_of_adult_skills_piaac_in_england.pdf)).

**Outlook.** The value of the skill shifts from *producing* text to *judging, editing and verifying* it, which is what the GCSE reading objectives already target. Over ten years, English is **augmented**: commodity writing jobs shrink, while reading, critical evaluation and oracy are strengthened in policy. Over 20–30 years **[SPEC]**, if routine text production is largely automated, the case for teaching writing rests on writing as thinking, civic and media literacy, and verification.

### Maths: answers have become cheap; number sense is how people check them

**Knowledge and skills.** The maths programme of study aims at fluency, reasoning and problem-solving. It states that calculators "should not be used as a substitute for good written and mental arithmetic" ([DfE Maths PoS](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study)). The CAR kept the volume of content but re-sequenced it: number fluency in KS1, multiplicative reasoning in KS2, and proportional and algebraic reasoning in KS3. It notes that pupils who cannot reason multiplicatively "rarely achieve grade 4". **31% of students do not reach grade 4 by 16**, and for disadvantaged (FSM) pupils the figure is 54% ([CAR](https://data.parliament.uk/DepositedPapers/Files/DEP2025-0723/Curriculum_and_Assessment_Review_final_report.pdf)). The CAR places AI in computing, not maths.

**AI capability [VENDOR, partly verified].** Google DeepMind's systems reached IMO silver in 2024 with human-formalised problems, later peer-reviewed in *Nature* ([Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/olympiad-level-formal-mathematical-reasoning-with-reinforcement-learning/)). Gemini Deep Think reached gold (35/42) in 2025, officially graded and working in natural language ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-google-deepmind-math-olympiad/)). Quanta describes a May 2026 counterexample to an Erdős conjecture as the first historically significant AI-originated proof ([Quanta](https://www.quantamagazine.org/how-ai-tore-through-a-mathematical-community-20260803/)). Terence Tao **[expert opinion, secondary]** estimates that only about 1–2% of open problems are currently AI-tractable with minimal human help, and favours human–AI collaboration ([Daily Maverick](https://dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-05-03-crossed-wires-what-does-the-worlds-greatest-living-mathematician-think-of-ai)). Third-party FrontierMath scores conflict and are unverified ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/frontiermath-tier-4-v2)). In practice, any GCSE or A-level question can now be solved by freely available AI.

**Risks.** Bastani's finding (a 17% drop in unaided exam scores) is a maths result. It lands hardest on the 31% who lack foundations, since they are the least able to spot AI errors. The calculator literature is a useful contrast. Meta-analyses found that calculators *integrated* with ordinary instruction generally did not harm paper-and-pencil skills. The exception was Grade 4, where sustained use appeared to harm basic skills ([Hembree via UT TRACE](https://trace.tennessee.edu/entities/publication/5f2f6fe3-1677-443e-b5f8-fab75cd70fce); [Ellington summary](https://www.math.odu.edu/~bogacki/epictcm/VOL13/C025/paper.pdf); both secondary summaries). The difference matters: calculators offload one deterministic step, while LLMs can complete the whole reasoning chain and still be wrong.

**Opportunities.** Scaffolded tutors that withhold answers (the Bastani guard-railed tutor; Kestin) suit "fluency through varied and frequent practice". That AI reduces maths anxiety is an untested hypothesis.

**Labour market and adult numeracy.** UK and US data point the same way:

- **Exposure [MODEL].** Maths-heavy work is highly exposed: maths and economics degrees lead to the most exposed UK jobs ([DfE 2023](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/656856b8cc1ec500138eef49/Gov.UK_Impact_of_AI_on_UK_Jobs_and_Training.pdf)).
- **Projected demand [MODEL].** US BLS projections for 2024–34 still show data scientists growing 34% and actuaries 22%, which is consistent with augmentation ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2026/it-adds-up-job-outlook-for-math-occupations.htm)).
- **Adult numeracy [EMP].** England scores 268 against an OECD average of 263, but about 21% of adults are at or below Level 1 ([GOV.UK PIAAC](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/survey-of-adult-skills-2023-national-report-for-england)). That is the population least able to check AI's numbers.
- **Reform [ADVOCACY / learned society].** The Royal Society proposes "mathematical and data education" and estimates 10–15 years to implement it fully ([Royal Society](https://royalsociety.org/news/2024/09/royal-society-calls-for-radical-reform-of-maths-education)).

**Outlook.** Over ten years, number fluency, estimation, proportional reasoning and statistics stay **needed** for learning and for checking AI. Routine calculation at work is largely **replaced**, and modelling and data interpretation are **augmented**. Over 20–30 years **[SPEC]**, basic numeracy for money, risk and health statistics is the most robust justification for teaching maths in any scenario.

### Science: AI predicts; humans still validate

**Knowledge and skills.** Science is organised into biology, chemistry and physics, with "working scientifically" (questioning, fair testing, measuring, analysing and evaluating) running through every key stage ([DfE Science PoS](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-science-programmes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-science-programmes-of-study)). GCSE requires 21 required practicals in Combined Science and 28 in the separate sciences, all assessed through written exams ([AQA](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/science/gcse/combined-science-trilogy-8464/specification/practical-assessment)). The CAR wants less content, clearly defined purposes for practicals, more climate science, and pupils who can "evaluate scientific claims critically" ([CAR](https://data.parliament.uk/DepositedPapers/Files/DEP2025-0723/Curriculum_and_Assessment_Review_final_report.pdf)).

**AI in research.** AI's clearest scientific success is prediction from well-curated data, recognised by the **2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry** for AlphaFold ([Nobel](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2024/press-release/)). Claims of autonomous *discovery* have repeatedly been challenged by domain experts:

- **GNoME.** Expert critique found "scant evidence" that its "materials" were novel, credible and useful ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2024/04/11/google_deepmind_material_study/)).
- **Berkeley A-Lab.** Its claimed new compounds were disputed, with problems traced to X-ray diffraction analysis ([Chemistry World](https://chemistryworld.com/news/new-analysis-raises-doubts-over-autonomous-labs-materials-discoveries/4018791.article)).
- **Sakana's "AI Scientist" [VENDOR].** Its peer-review result was a workshop acceptance only ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/12/sakana-claims-its-ai-paper-passed-peer-review-but-its-a-bit-more-nuanced-than-that)).

The Royal Society warns that opaque AI could make findings "less reliable" ([Royal Society](https://royalsociety.org/news/2024/05/ai-research-tools-could-undermine-trust-accuracy-scientific-findings/)).

**Risks.** The share of GCSE students doing hands-on practicals at least fortnightly fell from **44% in 2016 to 26% in 2023**, with video replacing them. That decline predates AI, and virtual labs could accelerate it ([Royal Society SET 2023](https://royalsociety.org/news/2024/04/science-education-tracker-2023/)). In 2023, 18% of GPT-4's citations were fabricated ([Walters & Wilder](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10484980/)). In an adversarial test, chatbots given malicious instructions produced health disinformation in 88 of 100 responses ([ASCO Post summary](https://ascopost.com/news/june-2025/study-finds-ai-chatbots-are-vulnerable-to-spreading-malicious-false-health-information/)).

**Opportunities.** The evidence is the Harvard physics tutor (0.73–1.3 SD) and the EEF finding of a 31% cut in planning time.

**Labour market.** UK engineering occupations are forecast to grow 1.6 times faster than non-priority sectors to 2030 ([RAEng Engineers 2030](https://raeng.org.uk/news/engineers-2030-final-report-launched/)). Hands-on science roles such as vets, radiographers and physiotherapists are among the least exposed ([DfE 2023](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/656856b8cc1ec500138eef49/Gov.UK_Impact_of_AI_on_UK_Jobs_and_Training.pdf)).

**Outlook.** Over ten years, conceptual knowledge is **needed** to direct and check AI. Data analysis is **augmented**, and fact recall and literature search are **partly replaced**. Practical skill and evaluating evidence become *more* valuable. Over 20–30 years **[SPEC]**, self-driving labs could take over routine screening, and scientific literacy for citizens becomes the main reason for compulsory science.

## Computing and D&T: coding is the most disrupted school skill, physical making the least

### Computing: routine code is automated; specification and verification remain

**Knowledge and skills.** The 2014 programme of study combines computer science, IT and digital literacy. It requires two programming languages at KS3, at least one of them textual, and does not mention AI ([DfE Computing PoS](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-computing-programmes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-computing-programmes-of-study)). GCSE Computer Science entries peaked at about 92,800 in 2024 and fell to **82,428 in 2026**. Girls are only **23%** of entrants **[ADVOCACY/DfE-funded data summary]** ([NCCE](https://teachcomputing.org/blog/gcse-results-2026)), compared with 43% for the old ICT GCSE in 2015 ([Schools Week](https://schoolsweek.co.uk/computing-revolution-led-to-stark-drop-in-girls-taking-subject)). Linking the recent fall in entries to AI is speculation. Entries fell in 18 of 31 GCSE subjects in 2025.

**AI capability and productivity.** AI coding has gone from near zero to near-saturated benchmarks. SWE-bench scores rose from **4.4% in 2023 to 71.7% in 2024** ([Stanford DEL, citing AI Index](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/canaries-in-the-coal-mine-six-facts-about-the-recen)). Reported 2026 scores of about 95% come from unofficial aggregators and are unverified ([BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/swe-verified)). Field productivity is much less clear:

| Evidence | Finding | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copilot lab study | 55.8% faster on a simple task | **[VENDOR]** | [Peng et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06590) |
| METR 2025 RCT | Experienced developers 19% *slower* on their own codebases | Independent | [METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/) |
| METR 2026 follow-up | Signal judged unreliable because developers refused to work without AI | Secondary | [conffab mirror](https://conffab.com/?p=10163) |
| Google, April 2026 | "75% of new code" AI-generated | **[VENDOR]**; method undisclosed | [Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/04/24/2026/google-ceo-says-75-of-companys-new-code-is-ai-generated) |

**Labour market.** The "canaries" junior-hiring signal is strongest in software. In the UK, the Institute of Student Employers reported tech graduate hiring down about 46% in 2025 ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/10/16/uk_tech_grad_jobs/)). A techUK panel argued that National Insurance rises explain at least as much ([techUK](https://www.techuk.org/resource/event-round-up-entry-level-and-graduate-jobs-in-the-era-of-ai.html)). PwC **[consultancy]** reports a 62% global wage premium for AI skills ([PwC 2026](https://pwc.com/gx/en/news-room/press-releases/2026/pwc-2026-ai-jobs-barometer.html)).

**Learning.** In a study of 21 novice programmers, strong students used AI to accelerate. Most of the strugglers reached solutions they could not have produced unaided, under an "illusion of progress" ([Prather et al., ICER 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17739v1)). The Raspberry Pi Foundation **[ADVOCACY]** argues that children still need to learn to code so that humans can "control these systems" ([RPF](https://static.raspberrypi.org/files/about/Why-kids-still-need-to-learn-to-code-in-the-age-of-AI-2025-Raspberry-Pi-Foundation-position-paper.pdf)).

**Opportunities.** AI literacy now has international frameworks. UNESCO published a student competency framework in 2024 ([UNESCO](https://raise.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/391105eng.pdf)). The EC–OECD AILit framework, finalised in June 2026, informs a **PISA 2029 Media & AI Literacy** assessment in which England will be benchmarked ([AILit](https://ailiteracyframework.org/)).

**Outlook.** Over ten years, computing is **augmented with partial replacement** of routine and junior coding. The skills that matter shift from writing syntax to specifying, reading, testing, security, data and ML literacy. At 20 years **[SPEC]**, if the 2047 median for human-level machine intelligence is right, professional programming could be largely automated, making computing **partly replaced as a job but needed as a literacy**. At 30 years **[SPEC]**, the case rests on civic understanding of what systems can and cannot do.

### Design and technology: AI enters the design phase; making and cooking remain human work

**Knowledge and skills.** D&T is organised around design, make, evaluate and technical knowledge, plus cooking and nutrition. It includes CAD and CAM and microcontrollers at KS3 ([DfE D&T PoS](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-design-and-technology-programmes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-design-and-technology-programmes-of-study)). Its decline predates AI. GCSE entries are down more than 80% from their 2001 peak ([LJMU](https://opendata.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/278/)) and fell to **84,320 in 2026**, with girls' entries down 7% **[ADVOCACY]** ([D&T Association](https://www.designtechnology.org.uk/news/gcse-results-day-2026/)). D&T remains a teacher-shortage subject ([NFER 2026](https://www.nfer.ac.uk/publications/school-teacher-labour-market-in-england-annual-report-2026/)). The CAR acknowledged the subject's "poor health" without mandating consistent provision ([D&T Association](https://www.designtechnology.org.uk/node/2231)). It renamed the food strand "Food and Nutrition" and added hygiene and sustainability ([BNF](https://foodafactoflife.org.uk/news/curriculum-and-assessment-review-final-report/)).

**Risks.** In a controlled study (n = 60), using an AI image generator during ideation **increased design fixation** and produced fewer, less varied and less original ideas ([CHI 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.11164)). Text-heavy coursework (NEA) folders are exposed to undisclosed AI writing. That GCSE D&T is about 50% NEA was not verified.

**Opportunities.** Small course studies report faster sketch-to-CAD cycles and more iterations ([arXiv 2510.19342](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.19342)). Rapid visualisation could also help pupils who cannot draw well.

**Labour market.** CITB projects a need for **41,200 extra construction workers a year to 2030**; one secondary source gives a different figure ([ACE/CITB](https://www.acenet.co.uk/news/infrastructure-intelligence/construction-must-find-206-000-extra-workers-by-2030-says-citb/)). McKinsey's estimates, via a secondary source, put automation potential at about 38% for unpredictable physical work against about 70% for predictable work ([ConstructConnect](https://www.constructconnect.com/blog/construction-technology/robots-coming-construction-site/)).

**Outlook.** Over ten years, design is **augmented**, while making, electronics and food are **needed**. At 20–30 years **[SPEC]**, the key unknown is general-purpose robotics. Repair, retrofit and bespoke work in unstructured settings is the likeliest to lag. Cooking competence remains needed for health whatever happens to robotics.

## Languages and humanities: translation is the most displaced skill, source criticism the most strengthened

### Languages: the clearest causal evidence of displacement, at the transactional end

**Knowledge and skills.** A language is compulsory at KS2 and KS3, with only an entitlement at KS4 ([DfE Languages PoS](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-languages-progammes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-languages-progammes-of-study)). The reformed GCSE in French, German and Spanish, first examined in 2026, is built on 1,200 (Foundation) and 1,700 (Higher) high-frequency words. It assesses dictation, reading aloud, translation in both directions and unprepared conversation, all without reference materials ([DfE GCSE MFL content](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67b60412ae5d020b0914bbdc/French__German__and_Spanish_GCSE_subject_content_without_Annexes_D_and_E_Feb_2025.pdf)). Uptake has been flat at about **44%**. French fell from 25% to 18% and German from 10% to 5%, while Spanish rose from 9% to 19%. Disadvantaged pupils take languages at 34% against 50% for others, and only 43% of the teacher-training recruitment target was met ([CAR](https://data.parliament.uk/DepositedPapers/Files/DEP2025-0723/Curriculum_and_Assessment_Review_final_report.pdf)). Uptake is more than 20 points higher in the most affluent schools ([British Council 2025](https://www.britishcouncil.org/research-insight/language-trends-england-2025)).

**Risks.** Machine translation is strong but limited. The WMT24 shared-task verdict was "The LLM Era is Here but MT is Not Solved Yet" ([Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/findings-of-the-wmt24-general-machine-translation-shared-task-the-llm-era-is-here-but-mt-is-not-solved-yet/)). GPT-4 matched junior translators but not medium or senior ones, and weakened for low-resource languages ([Yan et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03658)). GCSE French, German and Spanish are among the best-served language pairs, so AI can complete almost all written homework. The exams themselves are largely AI-resilient. That pupils lose motivation ("my phone translates") is plausible but has not been measured in England.

**Labour market.** This is the strongest causal displacement evidence of any subject **[EMP]**. In 695 US labour markets, Google Translate adoption meant about **28,000 fewer translator positions** were created over 2010–2023 than trend. Demand for foreign-language skills in job postings fell for all five languages studied. The effect on Chinese was muted in IT, science and engineering, and wages recovered after 2016. The study is an Oxford Martin working paper, not yet peer-reviewed ([Frey & Llanos-Paredes](https://www.oxfordmartin.ox.ac.uk/publications/lost-in-translation-artificial-intelligence-and-the-demand-for-foreign-language-skills)). In a Society of Authors survey, 36% of UK translators reported losing work to generative AI. This is **[SURVEY]**, self-selected and skewed towards literary translators ([The Bookseller](https://thebookseller.com/news/a-third-of-translators-report-losing-work-to-generative-ai-systems-soa-survey-reveals)). The US still projects +2% growth for interpreters and translators ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/media-and-communication/interpreters-and-translators.htm)).

**Opportunities.** The government will "explore how AI and education technology (edtech) can support stronger outcomes in language education" ([Government response](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/690b2a4a14b040dfe82922ea/Government_response_to_the_Curriculum_and_Assessment_Review.pdf)). An AI conversation partner limited to the GCSE vocabulary list is technically feasible and would address the shortage of speaking practice. Its effect on learning is untested. Professional bodies **[ADVOCACY]** argue that AI "strengthen[s] rather than weaken[s]" the case for languages ([CIOL](https://www.ciol.org.uk/impact-ai-language-industry-opportunities-and-challenges)). Pre-AI research found SMEs using language skills 30% more successful at exporting ([British Academy](https://www.thebritishacademy.ac.uk/news/british-academy-welcomes-new-research-showing-value-of-language-skills-to-smes/)).

**Outlook.** Over ten years, transactional translation is **partly replaced** and intercultural and relational use is **augmented**. School teaching time yields roughly A1–B1 competence, far below the FCDO's 450 hours to C1. That is exactly the level machine translation now covers, which sharpens the "why learn?" question. At 20–30 years **[SPEC]**, near-seamless speech translation for major languages is plausible. Professional translation would shrink to high-stakes, creative and low-resource niches, while the personal, cultural and trust value of speaking a language is, by definition, not substitutable.

### History: research synthesis is exposed; judging sources is strengthened

**Knowledge and skills.** History combines chronological knowledge with disciplinary concepts: evidence, interpretation, causation, change and significance ([DfE History PoS](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-history-programmes-of-study); wording not re-fetched). GCSE take-up is 42%. The CAR found the subject "broadly working well", but wants stronger knowledge of "how historians… construct historical claims". It explicitly links source assessment to media literacy and names deepfakes of "current or historical events" as disinformation ([CAR](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/690b96bbc22e4ed8b051854d/Curriculum_and_Assessment_Review_final_report_-_Building_a_world-class_curriculum_for_all.pdf)).

**Risks.** The American Historical Association warns that AI "regularly hallucinates content, references, sources, and quotations" and produces "texts… not truths" ([AHA](https://www.historians.org/event/guiding-principles-for-ai-in-history-education/)). Essay outsourcing threatens day-to-day learning more than GCSE grades, which stay exam-based.

**Opportunities.** Machine learning recovered Greek text from a carbonised Herculaneum scroll, winning the $700,000 Vesuvius Challenge grand prize ([Oxford Herculaneum](https://www.herculaneum.ox.ac.uk/index.php/2025/10/06/latest-news-from-the-vesuvius-challenge/)).

**The key finding for schools.** Historians' close, "vertical" reading of a source does not transfer to the web on its own. Professional fact-checkers, who read "laterally" by checking what other sources say, outperformed PhD historians **[EMP]**. Six one-hour lessons on lateral reading significantly improved US students' judgement of credibility in a field study (n = 271 treatment vs 228 comparison) ([Stanford GSE](https://ed.stanford.edu/news/it-doesn-t-take-long-learn-how-spot-misinformation-online-stanford-study-finds); [COR](https://cor.inquirygroup.org/research/lateral-reading-on-the-open-internet)). A Canadian study found gains that persisted ([HKS Misinformation Review](https://misinforeview.hks.harvard.edu/?p=6029)).

**Labour market.** Microsoft's analysis **[VENDOR]** reportedly ranks historians about second for AI applicability, with rankings reported via the press. The authors warn that reading this as job loss "would be a mistake" ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/article/what-are-the-jobs-most-exposed-to-ai-microsoft-research/)).

**Outlook.** Over ten years, history is **augmented**, and source criticism becomes more valuable. At 20–30 years **[SPEC]**, routine research synthesis is largely automated, and the human role concentrates on provenance, interpretation and accountability.

### Geography: AI builds the maps; fieldwork checks them against the ground

**Knowledge and skills.** Geography covers locational and place knowledge, physical and human processes, and enquiry skills, including GIS at KS3 and fieldwork in two contrasting environments at GCSE ([DfE Geography PoS](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-geography-programmes-of-study); not re-fetched). GCSE take-up rose to 41%. The CAR found fieldwork "inconsistently provided", posing a risk of "a significant skills gap". It names "notable gaps in relation to climate change" and wants "spatial reasoning, use of digital tools… and use of evidence" made explicit at KS3 ([CAR](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/690b96bbc22e4ed8b051854d/Curriculum_and_Assessment_Review_final_report_-_Building_a_world-class_curriculum_for_all.pdf)).

**Opportunities.** AI is transforming the discipline's tools. GraphCast beat ECMWF's operational forecast on 90% of 1,380 verification targets ([Science 2023](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adi2336); not re-fetched).

**Risks.** The risk is an inference rather than a measured effect: if scarce fieldwork is replaced with AI or virtual substitutes, the gap the CAR identified widens, especially for disadvantaged pupils. The research found no study on whether AI tools weaken pupils' spatial reasoning, and no GIS labour-demand data.

**Outlook.** Over ten years, geography is **augmented**. The scarce skill becomes judging whether a model's output is plausible on the ground. At 20–30 years **[SPEC]**, place knowledge and field judgement keep their value as checks on automated analysis.

## Citizenship, RE and RSHE: AI itself becomes part of the curriculum

### Citizenship: needed more as AI erodes trust in information

**Knowledge and skills.** Citizenship covers democracy, law, rights and personal finance. It is statutory only at KS3–4, and GCSE take-up is under 5% ([DfE Citizenship PoS](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-citizenship-programmes-of-study); [CAR](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/690b96bbc22e4ed8b051854d/Curriculum_and_Assessment_Review_final_report_-_Building_a_world-class_curriculum_for_all.pdf)). It becomes statutory from KS1, with media literacy defined to include messages "through different media channels, including AI", and is aimed at first teaching in September 2028 ([Westminster Brief PQ](https://westminsterbrief.co.uk/archive/pq/1386)).

**Risks.** The evidence calls for calm but serious attention. CETaS (Alan Turing Institute) found "no conclusive evidence" that AI disinformation changed 2024 election results, including the UK's. It did document confusion about what is authentic and deepfake abuse of candidates ([Turing written evidence](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/135409/default/)). Public skill is low. About 27% of UK adults feel confident spotting AI content, and only 9% of adults and 20% of 8–15-year-olds feel confident identifying deepfakes. These come from different surveys and are not directly comparable ([Ofcom 2024](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/__data/assets/pdf_file/0020/283025/adults-media-use-and-attitudes-report-2024.pdf); [UK Safer Internet Centre](https://saferinternet.org.uk/blog/ofcom-releases-research-into-ai-generated-content)). Nearly half of children who read AI search summaries trust them completely ([Ofcom 2026](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/research-and-data/media-literacy-research/children/2026-children-and-parents-report/children-and-parents-media-use-and-attitudes-report-2025-6.pdf?v=418231)).

**Opportunities.** The CAR's statutory expansion of citizenship and media literacy is the main vehicle. Lateral-reading instruction has trial evidence behind it (see History), though no AI-specific media literacy curriculum has been evaluated in English schools.

**Outlook.** The teaching goal is calibrating trust, not just detecting fakes. Over ten years citizenship is **needed and expanding**. At 20–30 years **[SPEC]**, its civic purpose is not a productivity task, though content such as AI governance and synthetic media will change continually. If votes at 16 are legislated, the first pupils taught this curriculum will vote in an information environment saturated with AI.

### RE: AI gives religious misrepresentation and ethics a place in the syllabus

**Knowledge and skills.** RE is a "basic curriculum" subject set locally by Agreed Syllabus Conferences under a framework largely unchanged since 1944. GCSE RS take-up is 31% ([CAR](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/690b96bbc22e4ed8b051854d/Curriculum_and_Assessment_Review_final_report_-_Building_a_world-class_curriculum_for_all.pdf)). The CAR recommends moving RE into the national curriculum through a task and finish group building on the RE Council's 2023 National Content Standard ([NATRE](https://natre.org.uk/news/the-curriculum-and-assessment-review-2025-implications-for-religious-education/)). The government's response makes this conditional on consensus in the sector.

**Risks.** Studies point to uneven representation of religion in AI models:

- **AllFaith benchmark (2026).** Testing 14 models, it reports that they offered religious perspectives on existential questions only 3% of the time, against 53% of people who value such perspectives. It also reports consistent preferences for some religions over others. This is reported via press coverage and is partly preprint ([Deseret News](https://www.deseret.com/faith/2026/05/26/studies-find-religious-bias-in-ai-models/)).
- **Earlier work.** Studies found persistent anti-Muslim bias in GPT-3 ([Nature Machine Intelligence](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-021-00359-2); not re-fetched) and Christianity "disproportionately" winning simulated interfaith debates ([arXiv 2407.06908](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2407.06908)).

**Opportunities.** The same evidence argues for teacher-led, knowledge-rich RE rather than chatbot-mediated learning about religions. AI ethics, covering bias, personhood, responsibility and the meaning of work, fits RE's philosophy and ethics strand.

**Outlook.** Over ten years RE is **needed**, with AI as content. At 20–30 years **[SPEC]**, questions of meaning and living together are not productivity tasks. The research found nothing indicating that the RE task and finish group will address AI.

### RSHE: the subject where AI's harms to children are already visible

**Knowledge and skills.** Revised RSHE guidance, published in July 2025 and in force from **1 September 2026**, adds "AI literacy", deepfake images, misogyny and incel culture, sextortion and personal safety ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/relationships-education-relationships-and-sex-education-rse-and-health-education); [PSHE Association](https://pshe-association.org.uk/news/rshe-guidance-2025-what-you-need-to-know)).

**Risks.** These are measured harms, not projections:

- **AI-generated child sexual abuse imagery.** The IWF identified **8,029** AI-generated child sexual abuse images and videos in 2025, with realism rising ([IWF 2025](https://www.iwf.org.uk/annual-data-insights-report-2025)).
- **Nude deepfakes.** 13% of UK teenagers have had some experience of nude deepfakes. The figure is 25% for vulnerable children against 11% for others ([Internet Matters](https://www.internetmatters.org/hub/press-release/new-report-uk-teenagers-encountered-ai-generated-nude-deepfakes/)).
- **Chatbots.** 64% of 9–17-year-olds use chatbots, and vulnerable children are more likely to use them for companionship ([Internet Matters](https://www.internetmatters.org/hub/research/me-myself-and-ai-chatbot-research/)).
- **Government response.** The government has committed to banning nudification apps ([VAWG Action Plan](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/697c7e21aacd0dc9777b502a/31.260_VAWG_02_Action_Plan_Slip_FINAL_v3_WEB_290126.pdf)), and Ofcom is investigating an AI companion service ([Ofcom](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/ofcom-investigates-ai-companion-chatbot-service)).

One gap needs checking. From the summaries reviewed, the guidance does not appear to name companion chatbots explicitly. If so, that is a content gap relative to the evidence, and it should be verified against the full guidance text.

**Opportunities.** Chatbots offer a "non-judgemental space to ask questions" and a way to rehearse difficult conversations. Neither use has been evaluated.

**Long-term value.** The Deming evidence on social skills, and OECD links between social-emotional skills and life outcomes ([OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2024/04/social-and-emotional-skills-for-better-lives_7af2f463.html)), suggest RSHE's relevance rises as AI automates codifiable cognitive work. The link between school PSHE lessons and adult social skills is indirect, however.

**Outlook.** RSHE is **needed and expanding** at every horizon. Confidence is high on the need and low on what the content will be.

## Art, music and PE: commodity output is exposed, live and physical practice is not

### Art and design: AI raises output but narrows originality

**Knowledge and skills.** Art and design covers drawing, painting and sculpture, sketchbook recording, critical evaluation and art history ([DfE Art PoS](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-art-and-design-programmes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-art-and-design-programmes-of-study)). Arts GCSE entries fell **42% between 2010 and 2023** **[ADVOCACY: Cultural Learning Alliance, using government data]** ([CfEY/CLA](https://cfey.org/reports/2024/05/cultural-learning-alliance-report-card-2024/)). The EBacc, a commonly cited cause, is being removed ([NSEAD](https://www.nsead.org/news/newsroom/ebacc-to-be-abolished/)).

**Risks.** Image-related freelancers on Upwork saw **−3.7% jobs and −9.4% earnings** after image generators launched **[EMP]**, and top performers were not protected ([Hui et al.](https://www.cesifo.org/DocDL/cesifo1_wp10601.pdf)). A quarter of illustrators in the Society of Authors survey reported losing work **[SURVEY]**. A 32% figure from the Association of Illustrators comes via a secondary source with an unverified sample ([Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/how-the-illustration-industry-is-grappling-with-ai-a-special-report/)).

**What AI does to creative work.** On an art-sharing platform with more than 4 million artworks, adopting text-to-image AI raised productivity by about **25%** and peer favourites by about 50%. **Average novelty fell**, and the human bottleneck moved to ideation and filtering ([Zhou & Lee, PNAS Nexus 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10914360)). Drawing improves memory in laboratory word-list studies, but the effect is inflated by study design and does not show classroom art benefits ([Fernandes et al.](https://fermatslibrary.com/s/the-surprisingly-powerful-influence-of-drawing-on-memory); [Jonker et al.](https://uwaterloo.ca/memory-attention-cognition-lab/sites/default/files/uploads/files/jonkerwammes19.pdf)).

**Copyright and the creative economy.** The UK's March 2026 copyright report proposed no broad commercial exemption for AI training, so licences remain necessary ([DSIT](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/693c70996a12691d48491f3d/Copyright_and_artificial_intelligence_DSIT_20ptfv.pdf)). The creative industries contributed £124bn (5.2%) of UK GVA in 2023 ([DCMS](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/dcms-economic-estimates-gva-2023-provisional/dcms-economic-estimates-annual-gva-2023-provisional)).

**Outlook.** Over ten years, art and design is **augmented and polarised**. Commodity illustration is partly replaced, while art direction, curation, judgement and physical craft hold their value. At 20–30 years **[SPEC]**, the economic case for human image-making narrows, and the cultural and developmental reasons for teaching it remain.

### Music: AI floods uploads, but live performance holds up

**Knowledge and skills.** Music covers performing, composing, listening, notation and history ([DfE Music PoS](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-music-programmes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-music-programmes-of-study)). The non-statutory Model Music Curriculum sets a minimum of an hour a week at KS1–2 ([MMC](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6061f833d3bf7f5ce1060a90/Model_Music_Curriculum_Full.pdf)). By 2022/23, 42% of schools entered no pupils for GCSE Music ([Music Teacher](https://www.musicteachermagazine.co.uk/news/article/report-finds-42-of-schools-no-longer-entering-students-for-gcse-music)), although entries rose 6.1% in 2025 ([Classic FM](https://www.classicfm.com/music-news/gcse-music-entries-rise-two-consecutive-years/)).

**Risks.** Deezer **[company-reported]** says fully AI-generated tracks exceeded **50% of daily uploads** by July 2026 but only 1–3% of streams, with most of those plays flagged as fraudulent ([DJ Mag](https://djmag.com/news/ai-generated-music-surpasses-50-of-daily-uploads-deezer)). A CISAC-commissioned projection **[ADVOCACY; MODEL]** puts 24% of music creators' revenues at risk by 2028 ([CISAC](https://www.cisac.org/cisacpmp-strategy-ai-study)). Universal and Warner settled with AI music firms in late 2025, while Sony was still litigating as of May 2026 ([Dubspot, secondary](https://blog.dubspot.com/suno-udio-ai-music-lawsuits-2026)). Composing coursework is now exposed, because Suno-type tools produce whole songs from a prompt.

**Live music.** UK music reached a record **£8bn** of GVA in 2024, helped by major tours ([Music Week](https://www.musicweek.com/labels/read/uk-music-report-values-industry-s-economic-contribution-at-8-billion-but-growth-slows-in-2024/093024)).

**The justification for music education.** High-quality meta-analyses find **no reliable far transfer** from music training to general cognition or attainment. The effect is g ≈ 0 once study quality is controlled for ([Sala & Gobet 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7683441)). Music education should therefore be justified by its cultural, social and performance value, not by "it makes you smarter".

**Outlook.** Over ten years, music is **augmented and polarised**: library and background composition is partly replaced, while live ensemble playing and teaching are needed. At 20–30 years **[SPEC]**, live, embodied music-making is very unlikely to be displaced as a human activity, but the recorded-music incomes of mid-tier creators are at risk.

### PE: the subject AI cannot do for pupils

**Knowledge and skills.** PE requires physical competence, sustained activity, competitive sport, outdoor activities, and swimming 25 metres with safe self-rescue ([DfE PE PoS](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-physical-education-programmes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-physical-education-programmes-of-study)). Only **49.1% of children** meet the Chief Medical Officer's guideline of 60 minutes of activity a day, the highest figure since 2017–18 ([Sport England](https://sportengland.org/news-and-inspiration/childrens-activity-levels-rising-inactivity-remains-too-high)). About 1 in 4 Year 6 pupils cannot swim 25m; the data year is not given ([Swim England](https://www.swimming.org/charity/support-us/a-nation-swimming/)).

**Exposure.** DfE ranks sports players as the **least AI-exposed occupation** ([DfE 2023](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/656856b8cc1ec500138eef49/Gov.UK_Impact_of_AI_on_UK_Jobs_and_Training.pdf)).

**Opportunities.** Technology-supported physical activity interventions generally raised activity levels in a review of 58 controlled studies ([Ha et al.](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/a-systematic-review-of-technology-infused-physical-activity-inter-2/)). Wearables appear to work best with teacher-led goal-setting, though that conclusion comes from a review of uncertain quality ([narrative review](https://internationalsped.com/index.php/ijse/article/download/4179/1176/14999)).

**Risks.** The main risk is indirect: screens and AI companions compete with time for activity. The research found no England-specific causal evidence for this.

**Outlook.** PE is **needed** at every horizon, with high confidence; AI only augments it, through motion analysis and differentiation. If AI reduces physical work, the health rationale for PE strengthens **[SPEC]**.

## Ten, twenty, thirty years: a scenario table

The table gives one verdict per subject at each horizon:

- **Needed:** humans must still be able to do it unaided.
- **Augmented:** still needed, but done with AI, and the valuable part shifts.
- **Partly replaced:** a large share of the *economic* task is done by AI. Learning the skill can still be justified.

The 10-year column extrapolates from 2022–26 evidence and official projections. The 20- and 30-year columns are **scenarios, not forecasts**. They assume AI capability somewhere between the expert-survey median for human-level machine intelligence (2047) and the much later median for full automation (2116) ([Grace et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02843)). A faster capability path would push most cognitive rows towards "partly replaced" by 2046. A slow-diffusion path would leave them "augmented".

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| English | Routine composition, summary | Critical reading, oracy, judgement | **Augmented**; foundation needed | Augmented; routine text largely automated | Needed as civic literacy and thinking tool | Moderate / Low / Very low |
| Maths | Procedural calculation, routine problems | Number sense, estimation, statistics, modelling | **Needed** (foundations) + augmented; routine calculation partly replaced at work | Augmented | Needed as civic numeracy | Moderate / Low / Very low |
| Science | Fact recall, literature search, data analysis | Practical work, evaluating evidence | **Augmented**; practical and evaluation more valuable | Augmented; routine lab screening partly replaced | Needed as scientific literacy | Moderate / Low / Very low |
| Computing | Writing routine code | Specification, verification, AI and data literacy | **Augmented, routine and junior coding partly replaced** | Partly replaced as a job; needed as literacy | Needed as civic literacy | Moderate / Low / Very low |

Several gaps limit every row:

- No UK school-age RCT of pupil-facing AI has reported.
- No study links generative-AI use to long-run skill trajectories beyond about two years.
- No forecast addresses demand for any school subject's skills at 20 or 30 years.
- No UK equivalent of the US payroll analysis exists.

## Conclusion

The evidence moves the question away from "which subjects will AI make obsolete?" Generative AI does not divide the curriculum into subjects that survive and subjects that don't. It cuts across every subject, separating the parts that produce outputs from the parts that build judgement, presence and relationships. In almost every subject, the most exposed strand (composition, procedural calculation, routine coding, transactional translation, commodity illustration) is also the one pupils most need to practise unaided in order to supervise AI later. The Bastani result and the jagged-frontier evidence make "AI can do it" a reason to keep teaching these skills, assessed without AI, not a reason to drop them. England's 2025–28 reforms have adopted this logic largely by default: exams over coursework, AI literacy in computing, media literacy in citizenship and English, and a stronger focus on number, oracy, fieldwork and practicals. They have not addressed the threat to junior entry routes into skilled work, which is the one labour-market effect consistently visible in the data.

The longer horizon calls for robustness rather than prediction. The expert-survey gap between 2047 and 2116 is too wide to plan a curriculum around any single date. Skills that hold value across every scenario are worth investing in now whichever future arrives: foundational literacy and numeracy used for verification, evidence evaluation, interpersonal and civic capacity, embodied competence, and the self-regulation needed to use AI without becoming dependent on it. Conversely, any claim that a specific school skill will be "needed" or "obsolete" in 2056 is not backed by research. The honest basis for answering the 20- and 30-year question is a set of scenarios, revisited as the evidence accumulates. The CAR's call for "more frequent updates" to digital curriculum content is the practical mechanism for doing that.
